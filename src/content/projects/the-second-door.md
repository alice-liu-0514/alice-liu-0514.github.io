## The Challenge

Justice-involved youth often need continuous and individualized social support. At the same time, frontline social workers face substantial administrative and documentation workloads.

Our first idea was a chatbot that would talk directly with young people, offering emotional support and connecting them to resources. Early fieldwork showed why that was the wrong place for AI: as an outside team we could not promise young people a lasting relationship; responsibility for an uncertain AI talking with minors was unclear; and round-the-clock alerts would have added to social workers’ load rather than reduced it.

We kept the goal, helping young people return to their communities, and changed the entry point. The project instead explored whether AI could reduce social workers’ repetitive administrative burden while preserving professional judgment and their relationships with young people.

## Understanding the Problem

The work began with conversations with people close to the frontline context, rather than with a technology-first solution. Between September and October 2025, we interviewed 9 frontline social workers and domain experts, including social workers and supervisors from youth-support organisations, the director of a youth centre, and academics, to understand youth reintegration challenges, documentation workflows, existing pain points, and opportunities and risks in using AI.

Social workers described four connected problems: writing visit records takes a great deal of time and crowds out other work; tight visit schedules leave no time to write up each visit; records pile up, in some cases up to a year behind; and the longer a record waits, the more details are lost or distorted.

We also ran an art therapy workshop with an art therapist for justice-involved youth. Organising it, which meant contacting more than six youth centres, taught us how much outside teams depend on trust with host organisations, and how irreplaceable in-person care from social workers is for young people.

## What We Built

We developed an AI-assisted record-keeping system to help practitioners organize case information and reduce repetitive documentation work. The system was designed to augment social workers’ capabilities, not replace their role or judgment.

- **Guided questions instead of a blank page.** Through a chat interface on LINE, the system asks 10 structured questions about a visit. Social workers answer in their own words and can go back and edit any answer.
- **AI organises, the social worker decides.** The AI arranges the answers into the sections of a standard visit record, such as observations, support systems, interventions, risk and safety, professional assessment, and follow-up, and adds a short summary that can be sent by email.
- **Target:** turn hours of writing into a guided process of about 10 to 15 minutes.

After the trial we kept refining it based on feedback: restricting the AI to information the social worker actually provided, and improving the professional wording of records.

## My Contribution

I led a four-person interdisciplinary team (psychology, electrical engineering and computer science, and engineering).

- Led the team and the project direction, including the pivot from a youth-facing chatbot to a tool for social workers.
- Organised and conducted interviews with 9 frontline social workers and domain experts.
- Translated interview findings into the system’s requirements and guided-question flow, working with our technical lead, who built the system.
- Coordinated the trial with social workers and collected 30 feedback responses within one week.

## What I Learned

The most important lesson from The Second Door was not simply that AI could make documentation faster.

Working with frontline practitioners taught me that efficiency alone does not make a technological intervention useful or responsible. A system must also fit professional practices, preserve human judgment, earn users’ trust, and reflect the lived realities of the people it is intended to support.

This experience shifted my focus from asking, “Can AI solve this problem?” to asking, “What role should AI play in human decision-making and relationships?” This question now guides much of my research in Human-AI Interaction.
