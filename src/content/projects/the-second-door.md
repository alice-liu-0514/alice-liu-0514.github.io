## The Challenge

Justice-involved youth often need continuous and individualized social support. At the same time, frontline social workers face substantial administrative and documentation workloads.

The project explored whether AI could reduce repetitive administrative burden while preserving professional judgment and human relationships.

## Understanding the Problem

The work began with conversations with people close to the frontline context, rather than with a technology-first solution. I interviewed 9 frontline social workers and domain experts to understand youth reintegration challenges, documentation workflows, existing pain points, and opportunities and risks in using AI.

## What We Built

We developed an AI-assisted record-keeping system to help practitioners organize case information and reduce repetitive documentation work. The system was designed to augment social workers’ capabilities, not replace their role or judgment.

## My Contribution

- Led the project team.
- Interviewed 9 frontline social workers and domain experts.
- Developed an AI-assisted record-keeping system for social workers.
- Collected 30 trial feedback responses within one week.

## What I Learned

The most important lesson from The Second Door was not simply that AI could make documentation faster.

Working with frontline practitioners taught me that efficiency alone does not make a technological intervention useful or responsible. A system must also fit professional practices, preserve human judgment, earn users’ trust, and reflect the lived realities of the people it is intended to support.

This experience shifted my focus from asking, “Can AI solve this problem?” to asking, “What role should AI play in human decision-making and relationships?” This question now guides much of my research in Human-AI Interaction.
